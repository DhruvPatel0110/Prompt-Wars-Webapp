PROMPT WARS 3-ROUND COMPETITION – COMPLETE EVENT ROUND STRUCTURE

============================================================ EVENT
CONCEPT ============================================================

PROMPT WARS is a competitive, team-based AI prompting event in which
participants progressively move from basic prompt engineering to
reverse-engineering AI-generated outputs and finally to solving a large
real-world case study under time pressure.

The competition is designed around one central idea:

    "Don't just use AI. Learn how to control the output."

The three rounds test three different abilities:

ROUND 1 – PROMPT MAKEOVER Can you transform a weak instruction into a
precise, powerful prompt?

ROUND 2 – PROMPT REVERSE ENGINEERING Can you look at an existing
AI-generated output and figure out the prompt that could have produced
it?

ROUND 3 – FINAL PROMPT BATTLE Can you build a complete solution and
adapt it instantly when the rules suddenly change?

============================================================ PARTICIPANT
FLOW ============================================================

Approximate starting participants: 100

Suggested team structure: - 3–4 students per team - Approximately 25–33
teams depending on final registration

ELIMINATION STRUCTURE:

ROUND 1 Approx. 100 participants | | 50% eliminated v ROUND 2 Approx.
50% of original participants | | 50% of Round 2 participants eliminated
v ROUND 3 Approx. 25% of original participants | | Final evaluation v
TOP 3 WINNING TEAMS

IMPORTANT: The exact number of teams retained can be adjusted depending
on the number of registered teams. Elimination should be based primarily
on score/rank rather than arbitrary selection.

============================================================ ROUND 1 –
PROMPT MAKEOVER “SPIN. UNLOCK. REWRITE.”
============================================================

PURPOSE

Round 1 is the entry-level but highly competitive prompt engineering
round.

Participants are first given a category through a spin/unlock system.
After the category is selected, they receive a bad or poorly written
prompt belonging to that category.

Their task is to transform the bad prompt into a strong, specific and
effective prompt.

The final prompt is evaluated and scored.

The weakest 50% of teams/participants are eliminated.

  ----------------------------------
  ROUND 1 – STEP 1: SPIN TO UNLOCK
  ----------------------------------

A spinner/wheel is displayed.

The wheel contains four category slots:

A B C D

A team comes forward or is selected to spin.

The result of the spin determines which category/genre group they will
receive.

The category should feel random so that participants cannot simply
prepare one fixed type of prompt beforehand.

Example:

    TEAM 7
       |
       v
    SPIN WHEEL
       |
       v
      "C"
       |
       v
    CATEGORY C
       |
       v
    GENRE SELECTED
       |
       v
    BAD PROMPT GIVEN

  ------------------------------------
  ROUND 1 – STEP 2: FOUR MAIN GENRES
  ------------------------------------

The four main genres should be broad enough to create variety while
still being understandable to undergraduate participants.

Suggested four main genres:

A – CREATIVE Examples: - Storytelling - Advertisement - Social media
content - Script writing - Poster/campaign concepts

B – BUSINESS Examples: - Marketing - Product launch - Business
strategy - Customer communication - Brand promotion

C – DATA / ANALYSIS Examples: - Reports - Tables - Financial summaries -
Data interpretation - Comparison and insights

D – REAL-WORLD / PROBLEM SOLVING Examples: - College problems - Event
planning - Productivity - Education - Public-awareness campaigns

NOTE: The four genres can be renamed to match the final event branding.
The important part is that the spin determines the genre and the genre
determines the bad prompt.

  ------------------------------
  ROUND 1 – STEP 3: BAD PROMPT
  ------------------------------

Once the genre is unlocked, the team receives a deliberately weak
prompt.

The bad prompt should be:

-   Vague
-   Missing context
-   Missing audience
-   Missing constraints
-   Missing output format
-   Open to multiple interpretations

The purpose is NOT to make the task impossible.

The purpose is to give participants enough room to demonstrate how well
they understand prompt engineering.

EXAMPLE 1 – CREATIVE

BAD PROMPT:

    "Make an advertisement for a college fest."

EXPECTED PARTICIPANT APPROACH:

Participants should identify that the prompt does not specify:

-   Target audience
-   Tone
-   Platform
-   Length
-   Event details
-   Key message
-   Call to action
-   Desired format

A possible improved prompt:

    "Act as a college marketing strategist. Create a 30-second
    promotional advertisement for an inter-college technology fest
    aimed at undergraduate students aged 18–22. Use an energetic,
    youthful and slightly humorous tone. Include a memorable tagline,
    three key attractions of the fest and a clear call to action.
    Present the result as a short video script with scene directions
    and dialogue."

EXAMPLE 2 – BUSINESS

BAD PROMPT:

    "Sell this product."

EXAMPLE 3 – DATA / ANALYSIS

BAD PROMPT:

    "Analyze this sales data."

EXAMPLE 4 – REAL-WORLD / PROBLEM SOLVING

BAD PROMPT:

    "Plan a college event."

The organizers should prepare multiple bad prompts in advance so that
different teams can receive different challenges.

  ----------------------------
  ROUND 1 – PARTICIPANT TASK
  ----------------------------

The team must:

1.  Read the assigned category.
2.  Understand the bad prompt.
3.  Identify what information is missing.
4.  Construct a substantially better prompt.
5.  Submit the final prompt before the deadline.

The participants are NOT required to produce the final answer
themselves.

The focus of this round is:

    BAD PROMPT -> GOOD PROMPT

  --------------------------
  ROUND 1 – SUGGESTED TIME
  --------------------------

Total: approximately 8–10 minutes per challenge.

Suggested structure:

1–2 minutes: Understand the challenge.

5–6 minutes: Construct and refine the prompt.

1–2 minutes: Final submission and checking.

A shorter time limit can be used if the number of teams is high.

  -------------------
  ROUND 1 – SCORING
  -------------------

Suggested total: 20 points.

1.  Clarity and specificity – 5 points
2.  Context and role definition – 4 points
3.  Constraints/instructions – 4 points
4.  Expected output format – 3 points
5.  Creativity and effectiveness – 4 points

TOTAL – 20 points

ALTERNATIVE: The organizers can actually run the submitted prompt
through the same AI model and award part of the score based on the
resulting output.

For example:

Prompt quality – 15 points Result/output quality – 5 points

This makes the round more practical and reduces the possibility of
participants receiving high scores merely for writing complicated
prompts.

  -----------------------
  ROUND 1 – ELIMINATION
  -----------------------

After all teams have submitted:

1.  Calculate the score of every team.
2.  Rank teams by total score.
3.  Retain approximately the top 50%.
4.  Eliminate the remaining teams.

The surviving teams proceed to ROUND 2.

For example:

100 participants -> 50 participants survive

============================================================ ROUND 2 –
PROMPT REVERSE ENGINEERING “SEE THE OUTPUT. BUILD THE PROMPT.”
============================================================

PURPOSE

Round 2 completely changes the direction of the competition.

In Round 1:

    PROMPT -> AI -> OUTPUT

In Round 2:

    OUTPUT -> ??? -> PROMPT

Participants are shown ready-made outputs.

They must study the output and create a prompt that could generate
something as close as possible to that output.

There are TWO separate challenges.

  -----------------------
  ROUND 2 – CHALLENGE 1
  GENERATED IMAGE
  -----------------------

Participants are shown a ready-made AI-generated image.

The image should be related to a product, scheme, campaign or commercial
concept.

Examples:

-   A government/public-awareness scheme poster
-   A new product advertisement
-   A college campaign
-   A sustainability campaign
-   A financial-awareness poster
-   A fictional product launch
-   A social-awareness campaign

EXAMPLE IMAGE BRIEF:

An AI-generated promotional poster showing a fictional student
scholarship scheme, with students, a modern educational background, a
large scheme title, supporting text, a call-to-action section and
specific visual styling.

IMPORTANT: Participants receive ONLY the finished image.

They do NOT receive the original prompt.

  --------------------------
  ROUND 2 – TASK FOR IMAGE
  --------------------------

Participants must write a prompt that would generate the displayed image
as accurately as possible.

They should think about:

-   Subject
-   Composition
-   Objects
-   People
-   Positioning
-   Background
-   Typography/text
-   Color/style
-   Lighting
-   Mood
-   Camera/view
-   Layout
-   Aspect ratio
-   Intended purpose

The objective is:

    "RECREATE THE OUTPUT THROUGH PROMPTING."

  -------------------------
  ROUND 2 – IMAGE EXAMPLE
  -------------------------

READY-MADE OUTPUT:

A promotional poster for a fictional student scholarship scheme.

The image contains: - Two college students - A university-style
background - A large title - A scholarship amount - Supporting
information - A registration call-to-action - Clean modern design -
Portrait poster layout

PARTICIPANT’S JOB:

Write the prompt that would generate this visual.

They are NOT judged on whether their prompt sounds complicated.

They are judged on whether the prompt accurately captures the important
visual characteristics of the target.

  ---------------------------------
  ROUND 2 – CHALLENGE 2
  FINANCIAL YEAR / MONTHLY REPORT
  ---------------------------------

Participants are shown a ready-made financial report.

The report is divided month-by-month across a financial year.

For example:

    APRIL
    MAY
    JUNE
    JULY
    ...
    MARCH

The report may contain:

-   Monthly sales
-   Monthly expenses
-   Profit/loss
-   Totals
-   Financial-year summary
-   Tables
-   Categories
-   Numerical values
-   Growth or comparison information

The exact numbers should be prepared by the organizers in advance.

  -------------------------------------
  ROUND 2 – TASK FOR FINANCIAL REPORT
  -------------------------------------

Participants must create a prompt that would generate the displayed
financial report.

The prompt should specify the necessary:

-   Financial year
-   Month structure
-   Sales values
-   Expense values
-   Categories
-   Calculations
-   Table structure
-   Totals
-   Formatting
-   Summary
-   Required output type

The challenge is essentially:

    GIVEN REPORT -> REVERSE ENGINEER -> ORIGINAL-STYLE PROMPT

  --------------------------------
  ROUND 2 – TWO SEPARATE PROMPTS
  --------------------------------

There should be TWO separate submission windows.

WINDOW 1: IMAGE REVERSE ENGINEERING

Participants study the image and submit one prompt.

WINDOW 2: FINANCIAL REPORT REVERSE ENGINEERING

Participants study the financial report and submit a second prompt.

The two prompts are scored separately and then combined.

  ----------------------------
  ROUND 2 – IF TIME IS SHORT
  ----------------------------

If the organizers have limited time, participants may be asked to
attempt ONLY ONE of the two challenges.

The organizers can either:

OPTION A: Give every team the same challenge.

OR

OPTION B: Randomly assign one challenge to each team.

If only one challenge is used, scoring should be normalized so that
teams attempting the image and teams attempting the financial report are
evaluated using equivalent scoring standards.

  -------------------
  ROUND 2 – SCORING
  -------------------

The key word for this round is:

    ACCURACY

Suggested total: 30 points.

IMAGE PROMPT – 15 points

1.  Subject/object accuracy – 3
2.  Layout/composition – 3
3.  Style/visual details – 3
4.  Text/content requirements – 3
5.  Overall similarity – 3

FINANCIAL REPORT PROMPT – 15 points

1.  Correct financial-year structure – 3
2.  Monthly data accuracy – 3
3.  Sales/expense details – 3
4.  Table/calculation structure – 3
5.  Overall output similarity – 3

TOTAL – 30

  ---------------------------------------
  ROUND 2 – IMPORTANT JUDGING PRINCIPLE
  ---------------------------------------

Participants should NOT receive high marks simply for writing a long
prompt.

A 150-word prompt that generates an inaccurate result should score lower
than a concise prompt that reproduces the target accurately.

The question for judges should be:

    "How closely does the generated result match the target output?"

  --------------------------
  ROUND 2 – SUGGESTED FLOW
  --------------------------

STEP 1: Display the ready-made image.

STEP 2: Give participants a fixed time to study it.

STEP 3: Open the IMAGE PROMPT submission window.

STEP 4: Collect prompts.

STEP 5: Display the financial report.

STEP 6: Give participants time to study it.

STEP 7: Open the FINANCIAL REPORT PROMPT submission window.

STEP 8: Run/evaluate the prompts.

STEP 9: Calculate total scores.

STEP 10: Rank all teams.

STEP 11: Eliminate approximately 50% of the remaining teams.

EXAMPLE:

50 participants -> 25 participants survive

============================================================ ROUND 3 –
FINAL PROMPT BATTLE “BUILD. ADAPT. SURVIVE.”
============================================================

PURPOSE

This is the final and most important round.

The remaining teams are given a large, realistic case study.

Unlike Round 1, participants are not merely improving a sentence.

Unlike Round 2, they are not reverse-engineering an existing output.

They must use AI prompting to create a complete solution.

  --------------------------
  ROUND 3 – THE CASE STUDY
  --------------------------

The case study should be large enough to require:

-   Understanding
-   Planning
-   Analysis
-   Creativity
-   Prompt engineering
-   Output structuring
-   Decision-making

The case should contain multiple constraints.

EXAMPLE CASE STUDY:

    COLLEGE EVENT PARTICIPATION CRISIS

    A college is organizing a major student festival in 30 days.

    Current situation:

    - Expected capacity: 1,000 students
    - Previous participation: 350 students
    - Target participation: 800 students
    - Marketing budget: Rs. 15,000
    - Promotion period: 30 days
    - Student volunteers: 20
    - Social media engagement is low
    - Competing college events are happening during the same period

    The organizers want a complete strategy to increase participation.

    Your team must develop an AI-assisted plan covering:

    1. Target audience
    2. Marketing strategy
    3. Content strategy
    4. Event promotion
    5. Budget allocation
    6. Timeline
    7. Student engagement
    8. Measurement/KPIs
    9. Risk management
    10. Expected outcomes

The actual case study can be changed according to the event theme.

  ----------------------------
  ROUND 3 – PARTICIPANT TASK
  ----------------------------

Teams must construct a MASTER PROMPT.

The master prompt should instruct AI to produce a complete solution to
the case.

The team should consider:

-   AI role/persona
-   Case context
-   Objective
-   Target audience
-   Constraints
-   Required tasks
-   Data
-   Assumptions
-   Output format
-   Evaluation criteria
-   Priorities
-   Desired level of detail

The aim is:

    CASE STUDY
         |
         v
    MASTER PROMPT
         |
         v
    AI OUTPUT
         |
         v
    COMPLETE SOLUTION

  ---------------------
  ROUND 3 – THE CLOCK
  ---------------------

Give the teams a fixed amount of time.

Suggested:

    15–20 minutes

The time should be long enough to understand the case and build a strong
prompt, but short enough to create pressure.

Suggested timeline:

0–5 minutes: Read and understand the case.

5–12 minutes: Build the master prompt.

12–17 minutes: Test/refine the prompt.

Final minutes: Prepare the final submission.

============================================================ THE FINAL
BOMB ============================================================

This is the signature feature of the final round.

JUST BEFORE THE ORIGINAL TIME LIMIT ENDS, the organizers announce:

    "NEW INFORMATION."

A sudden change is introduced into the case.

The teams now have approximately 30 SECONDS to modify their prompt.

They cannot rebuild their entire solution from scratch.

They must rapidly identify what needs to change and adapt their prompt.

EXAMPLE BOMB #1:

    BUDGET CUT!

    Your original budget of Rs. 15,000 has suddenly been reduced
    to Rs. 5,000.

    ADAPT YOUR PROMPT.

EXAMPLE BOMB #2:

    AUDIENCE CHANGE!

    Your target audience has changed from college students to
    parents and guardians.

    ADAPT YOUR PROMPT.

EXAMPLE BOMB #3:

    PLATFORM BAN!

    Social media promotion is no longer permitted.

    ADAPT YOUR PROMPT.

EXAMPLE BOMB #4:

    TIME CRISIS!

    The event has been moved forward by 15 days.

    ADAPT YOUR PROMPT.

EXAMPLE BOMB #5:

    RESOURCE LOSS!

    Half of your volunteers are no longer available.

    ADAPT YOUR PROMPT.

  -----------------
  FINAL BOMB RULE
  -----------------

The bomb should NOT introduce an entirely unrelated problem.

It should modify one important condition of the original case.

This ensures that the challenge tests:

    ADAPTABILITY

rather than simply rewarding teams that can type quickly.

  ----------------------------------
  ROUND 3 – 30-SECOND FINAL WINDOW
  ----------------------------------

Once the bomb is announced:

    30 SECONDS

Teams must:

1.  Understand the new condition.
2.  Identify the affected part of their prompt.
3.  Modify the prompt.
4.  Submit the final version.

No major rewriting should be allowed after the 30-second window closes.

============================================================ ROUND 3 –
FINAL EVALUATION
============================================================

Suggested total: 50 points.

MASTER PROMPT – 30 POINTS

1.  Understanding of case – 5
2.  Context and objective – 5
3.  Instructions and constraints – 5
4.  Structure/output requirements – 5
5.  Creativity and strategic thinking – 5
6.  Prompt effectiveness – 5

FINAL BOMB RESPONSE – 20 POINTS

1.  Correct adaptation to new condition – 5
2.  Speed and precision – 5
3.  Preservation of original objective – 5
4.  Quality of final AI output – 5

TOTAL – 50

============================================================ FINAL TOP 3
SELECTION ============================================================

The final round should be judged using the final submitted prompt and
the resulting AI output.

The highest-scoring three teams are declared:

    TOP 3 WINNING TEAMS

Suggested final result:

    1st Place – PROMPT WARS CHAMPION
    2nd Place – RUNNER-UP
    3rd Place – SECOND RUNNER-UP

The organizers can decide whether the final three are ranked purely
through the final-round score or through a combination of Round 3
performance and previous-round performance.

RECOMMENDED: Use Round 3 as the dominant factor because it is the final
and most comprehensive test.

============================================================ COMPLETE
EVENT SUMMARY
============================================================

ROUND 1 PROMPT MAKEOVER “SPIN. UNLOCK. REWRITE.”

INPUT: A random genre + bad prompt

TASK: Turn the bad prompt into a powerful prompt.

SKILL TESTED: Basic prompt engineering

APPROXIMATE SURVIVAL: 50%

                ↓

ROUND 2 PROMPT REVERSE ENGINEERING “SEE THE OUTPUT. BUILD THE PROMPT.”

INPUT: 1. Ready-made AI-generated image 2. Ready-made monthly
financial-year report

TASK: Write prompts capable of generating the displayed outputs.

SKILL TESTED: Observation + reverse engineering + accuracy

APPROXIMATE SURVIVAL: 50% of Round 2 participants

                ↓

ROUND 3 FINAL PROMPT BATTLE “BUILD. ADAPT. SURVIVE.”

INPUT: Large real-world case study

TASK: Create a master prompt that generates a complete solution.

FINAL TWIST: A sudden “bomb” changes one major condition approximately
30 seconds before the deadline.

TASK AFTER BOMB: Rapidly modify the prompt and generate the adapted
solution.

SKILL TESTED: Advanced prompt engineering + strategic thinking +
adaptability under extreme time pressure

FINAL RESULT: TOP 3 TEAMS

============================================================ EVENT
PHILOSOPHY ============================================================

The three rounds should deliberately increase in difficulty.

ROUND 1: Participants are told what is wrong.

    BAD PROMPT
         |
         v
    FIX THE PROMPT

ROUND 2: Participants are given only the result.

    OUTPUT
       |
       v
    FIGURE OUT THE PROMPT

ROUND 3: Participants are given a complex problem.

    PROBLEM
       |
       v
    DESIGN THE PROMPT
       |
       v
    GENERATE SOLUTION
       |
       v
    SURVIVE THE BOMB

This creates a clear progression:

    ROUND 1 = CREATE
    ROUND 2 = REVERSE ENGINEER
    ROUND 3 = ADAPT

The final message of PROMPT WARS becomes:

    "Anyone can ask AI a question.
     The real skill is knowing how to ask the right question."

============================================================ TIE-BREAKER
RULE ============================================================

If two or more teams have the same score at an elimination boundary, use
a short tie-breaker.

Suggested tie-breaker:

Give teams ONE bad prompt and only 60 seconds.

They submit a single improved prompt.

Judges evaluate prompt effectiveness.

The higher-scoring prompt advances.

============================================================ IMPORTANT
FAIRNESS RULES
============================================================

1.  All teams in the same round should receive challenges of comparable
    difficulty.

2.  The same AI model/version should be used for comparable evaluations.

3.  Participants should know the time limit before the round begins.

4.  The final bomb should be announced simultaneously to all teams.

5.  Judges should use a predefined scoring rubric.

6.  Prompt length alone should never determine the score.

7.  In Round 2, accuracy/similarity to the target output should be the
    primary consideration.

8.  In Round 3, judges should evaluate both the original strategy and
    the team’s ability to adapt to the bomb.

9.  Organizers should test all target outputs before the event.

10. A clear tie-breaker should be established before the competition
    begins.

DESCRIPTIONS FOR EVENT HOSTING
============================================================

ROUND 1: “Spin your genre, face a terrible prompt, and turn it into
something AI can actually understand.”

ROUND 2: “We show you the answer. You figure out the question.”

ROUND 3: “Build the perfect AI strategy. Then, when you think you’re
safe, we change the rules.”
